## Nonasymptotic Local Minimax Bounds for Decision-Dependent Stochastic Optimization

*Master's Thesis Research, Johns Hopkins University · Mar. 2026–Present*

Supervised by [Prof. Mateo Díaz](https://mateodd25.github.io/) (University of Chicago) and [Prof. Liwei Jiang](https://liwei-jiang97.github.io/) (Purdue University).

I study finite-sample statistical guarantees for decision-dependent stochastic optimization, where decisions influence the distribution of subsequent observations. The project asks how accurately an equilibrium can be estimated from finitely many samples, what the corresponding local minimax benchmark is, and whether an efficient stochastic optimization method can attain it.

## Anytime-Valid Bounds for Clipped Gradient Descent

*Research Assistant, Johns Hopkins University · Jan. 2026–Apr. 2026*

Supervised by [Prof. Mateo Díaz](https://mateodd25.github.io/) (University of Chicago).

I study time-uniform, high-probability convergence guarantees for stochastic gradient methods. The analysis covers convex, nonconvex, and quadratic-growth settings and uses martingale concentration and mixture-based arguments. I also analyze clipped stochastic gradient descent under stochastic noise assumptions.

## Diffusion MRI-Based Microstructural Modeling for Chronic Ischemic Stroke Data

*Research Assistant, Shenzhen Research Institute of Big Data · Jul. 2024–Sep. 2025*

Supervised by [Prof. Xiang Wan](https://www.sribd.cn/en/teacher/28).

I formulated and implemented biophysical diffusion MRI models for estimating latent cellular microstructure, developed numerical model-fitting and parameter-estimation procedures, and conducted longitudinal statistical and imaging–clinical–proteomic association analyses.

*Manuscript submitted to [Stroke](https://www.ahajournals.org/journal/str); under review.*

### Multimodal Medical Image Segmentation with Deep Learning

I modified the U-Mamba architecture to improve long-range dependency modeling, integrated medical text annotations with imaging features for multimodal segmentation, and evaluated the resulting model relative to the LViT baseline.
