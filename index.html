Applied Mathematics · Statistics · Optimization

Yangfan Sun

I am an M.S.E. student in Applied Mathematics and Statistics at Johns Hopkins University. My research lies at the intersection of optimization, statistics, and machine learning theory, with a particular interest in understanding the mathematical structure of learning and decision problems and using that structure to develop efficient methods with rigorous guarantees. My current work focuses on nonasymptotic local minimax theory for decision-dependent stochastic optimization and anytime-valid guarantees for stochastic gradient methods.

Research

Optimization, statistical theory, and reliable learning methods.

Nonasymptotic Local Minimax Bounds for Decision-Dependent Stochastic Optimization

Master's Thesis Research · Johns Hopkins University

Mar. 2026–Present

Supervised by Prof. Mateo Díaz (University of Chicago) and Prof. Liwei Jiang (Purdue University).

I study finite-sample statistical guarantees for decision-dependent stochastic optimization, develop stochastic optimization methods for estimating equilibrium points, and investigate matching local minimax lower bounds to characterize nonasymptotic statistical optimality.

Anytime-Valid Bounds for Clipped Gradient Descent

Research Assistant · Johns Hopkins University

Jan. 2026–Apr. 2026

Supervised by Prof. Mateo Díaz (University of Chicago).

I study time-uniform, high-probability convergence guarantees for stochastic gradient methods, including convex, nonconvex, and quadratic-growth settings, using martingale concentration and mixture-based techniques. I also analyze clipped stochastic gradient descent under stochastic noise assumptions.

Diffusion MRI-Based Microstructural Modeling for Chronic Ischemic Stroke Data

Research Assistant · Shenzhen Research Institute of Big Data

Jul. 2024–Sep. 2025

Supervised by Prof. Xiang Wan.

Manuscript submitted to Stroke; under review.

I formulated and implemented biophysical diffusion MRI models for estimating latent cellular microstructure, developed numerical model-fitting and parameter-estimation procedures, and conducted longitudinal statistical and imaging–clinical–proteomic association analyses.

Multimodal Medical Image Segmentation with Deep Learning

I modified the U-Mamba architecture to improve long-range dependency modeling, integrated medical text annotations with imaging features, and improved segmentation performance relative to the LViT baseline.
